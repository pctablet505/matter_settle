Standard / rhythm / terms

How we work

The engineering standard we hold ourselves to, the rhythm of a week, and the terms we start from. You can check all of it during a small first project.

01 / The standard

The difference between a notebook and a system

None of this is new. It is what a system needs when someone has to run it on an ordinary Tuesday.

  1. S1 Tests gate the merge

    The paths that matter are pinned by tests that fail loudly when behaviour changes, so nothing drifts into a release unnoticed.

  2. S2 Unseen data

    Results are measured on data the model has never seen, across time windows where time matters. The unflattering number is reported next to the good one.

  3. S3 Real costs

    Fees, delays, rate limits and bad rows are part of the problem. A result that ignores them describes a world that does not exist.

  4. S4 Runs unattended

    Checkpoints, retries, quality gates and a fallback source, because the part that breaks in production is rarely the model. See data pipelines.

  5. S5 Monitoring is the job

    Shipping is not the end of the work. Knowing when a system has quietly stopped being right is part of it.

The same standard, turned into checks you can apply to your own system: the production-readiness checklist.

02 / A week with us

Something you can run, every week

Each week ends with something you can run yourself, not a status deck. It might be a pipeline run on real data, a service you can call, or an evaluation you can repeat. You can execute it, read the tests, and form your own view of whether it works.

The code lands in your repository and runs in your accounts from the first commit. You see the work while it happens, not after it is sold.

You talk to the engineer writing the code, not an account manager. We work in India Standard Time, which overlaps European working hours and US mornings.

03 / How an engagement runs

Four steps, and you can stop after the second

  1. Step 01

    Scope it in writing

    The deliverable, the price and an agreed meaning of “done”, agreed before any money moves. If AI is the wrong tool, we say so now, and you owe us nothing.

  2. Step 02

    A paid pilot

    Fixed price, fixed scope, one clear deliverable. Its length and price are part of the written scope.

  3. Step 03

    Build in the open

    Weekly, runnable progress in your repository. You judge it as it grows.

  4. Step 04

    Ship, then support

    Deployment and monitoring: the half of the work that keeps a system right after launch.

04 / The terms

The terms we start from

They describe how we intend to work with you, not what happened in the past. You find out whether we keep them during the first small project, not after a large one.

  1. T1 Fixed pilot

    A fixed-price, fixed-scope pilot with one clear deliverable, agreed in writing before it starts.

  2. T2 Your code

    Your repository and your accounts from the first commit. Nothing to extract later, and no lock-in to negotiate out of.

  3. T3 Weekly

    Something runnable every week, not a status deck.

  4. T4 Walk away

    If the pilot does not convince you, you keep everything it produced and the work stops there.

  5. T5 Direct access

    You work with the engineer doing the work. No account layer, and no hand-off to people you never met.
