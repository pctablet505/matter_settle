02 / How a pilot works

Start small, in writing

A pilot is the first small piece of real work. Its scope and price are agreed in writing before it starts.

  1. Step 01

    Scope it

    We agree the deliverable and what “done” means. If AI is the wrong tool, we say so here.

  2. Step 02

    Run a paid pilot

    One small deliverable, at a price set before it starts.

  3. Step 03

    Build in the open

    You run each week's version yourself.

  4. Step 04

    Ship, then support

    Deployment and monitoring: the half of the work that keeps a system right.

05 / When we say no

When AI is the wrong tool

We will tell you. If a rule, a database query or a spreadsheet would do the job, you hear it at the scoping stage, and you owe us nothing.

We also say no when there is no data to learn from, when nobody can say what a good result looks like, or when the work is outside what we do.

07 / Questions

Four short questions

The longer list is on the questions buyers ask page.

How much does a pilot cost, and how long does it take?

Both depend on the problem, so both are set in the written scope before anything starts. You know the price before you commit.

Who owns the code?

You do, from the first commit. It lives in your repository and runs in your accounts.

Will we talk to the person doing the work?

Yes. You talk to the engineer writing the code, not an account manager.

What do you need from us to start?

Two or three sentences about what you are building. If it fits, a short call, then a written scope.
